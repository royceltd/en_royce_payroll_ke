# Research notes, 2026-10-22: HRMS, Navari csf_ke, Kenyan PAYE rules

Findings behind the v16.4.0 engine (`taxable-income-engine.md`) and the improvement plan
below. Sources: local HRMS v16.51.0 and v16.20.1 source, Frappe HR docs and release notes,
Navari `csf_ke` v16.12.0 (`~/Downloads/navari_csf_ke`, branch `version-16`), and KRA.

## 1. How HRMS payroll works (what matters for Kenya)

- **Salary Component flags**:
  - *Is Tax Applicable* (earnings): counts as taxable pay.
  - *Exempted from Income Tax* (deductions): taken off taxable pay before tax.
  - Both are read **only** by HRMS's own tax engine: a deduction with *Variable Based On
    Taxable Salary* plus a linked Income Tax Slab, and for the exempted flag only when the slab
    has *Allow Tax Exemption* ticked (`salary_slip.py` `get_taxable_earnings`).
- **HRMS's tax engine is India-style and annualised**: it projects the year's taxable pay,
  computes annual tax on the slab, subtracts tax already paid and spreads the rest over the
  remaining months. It also can't express caps (it subtracts the full exempted amount), partial
  exemptions or Kenyan reliefs. **Kenya PAYE is monthly**, so we keep that engine off. The
  flags' *meaning* fits Kenya exactly; the engine behind them doesn't.
- **`gross_pay`** = every earning not marked *Do Not Include in Total*, including Additional
  Salary, computed before deductions are evaluated (`calculate_net_pay`).
- **Formula context**: all component abbreviations default to 0, plus Salary Structure
  Assignment fields, Employee fields and slip fields (`hrms.payroll.utils.get_component_eval_context`).
  Salary Structures keep their own **copy** of each formula; editing the component master
  doesn't change existing structures.
- **Part-month proration**: `update_component_row` re-applies payment-days proration to any row
  with *Depends on Payment Days*. Applied to statutory deductions, that halves a minimum or
  limit for a mid-month joiner (the bug v16.4.0 fixes).
- **Extension point**: `SalarySlip.apply_regional_deductions()`, decorated
  `@hrms.allow_regional`, runs after earnings and deductions and before totals. It's overridden
  per country through `regional_overrides` (region = Company country or System Settings
  country). Present on 16.20.1 and 16.51. This is what the Royce engine uses.
- **v16 features to build on**:
  - *Employer Contribution* component type (v16.12), shown in its own section of the slip
    (v16.18), with a liability account and a separate journal entry (v16.20).
  - *Arrear* and *Payroll Correction* records, which create Additional Salary.
  - *Salary Withholding*, *Bulk Salary Structure Assignment*, the *Employee CTC Break-up* report.
- **Loans**: HRMS has none of its own; it uses the separate **Lending** app (built for banks
  and microfinance lenders: provisioning rules, NPA classification, co-lenders, loan
  securities). Slips deduct open loans with *Repay from Salary*. Lending is on the local bench
  but not in `release.yml`. **Decision: don't adopt it**; build a light Staff Loan later.

## 2. Navari csf_ke: what it is and what we took

- **No calculation engine.** Payroll is: two spreadsheets in the repo root (`Salary
  Component.xlsx`, about 137 components; `Salary Structure.xlsx`, "NSSF Structure 2025" for
  "Demo Co") imported by hand; custom fields (P9A/P10A mapping, KRA PIN, NSSF and NHIF numbers,
  bank codes); and reports (P9A, P10, NSSF, SHIF, Housing Levy, HELB, bank advice, payroll
  register, period-to-period comparison). The spreadsheets look like one client's exported
  setup (Madison Insurance, Chamaa, old NHIF and pre-COVID bands).
- **Their calculation chain, in formulas:**
  1. `gross_pay` is redefined as a component with that abbreviation (a hand-typed list of
     earnings), which overrides HRMS's own `gross_pay` in later formulas.
  2. NSSF uses ten tier/band components on Basic only.
  3. `SOPF` (sum of NSSF + provident funds) is capped at `MPF` (20,000) and gives `PFR`.
  4. `TI` = hand-typed taxable earnings − PFR − SHIF − Housing Levy.
  5. Bands give `GROSSIT`, minus insurance relief `IRLF` (15% of `INS`), minus personal relief.
  6. Extras: Board PAYE (flat 30% for directors) and a disability exemption (`PTE` 150,000).
- **Borrowed (as ideas, not code; csf_ke is GPL-3):**
  - *Taxable earnings ≠ gross*: tax only what is taxable. We do it with flags instead of a
    typed list.
  - *Pension pooling with a cap*: NSSF plus other pension schemes under one limit.
  - *Insurance relief*, *disability exemption*, and a *director / secondary* tax route.
  - Report ideas for later: HELB, payroll register, **month-on-month variance**.
  - The P9A/P10A mapping fields, earlier, under the `royce_` prefix.
- **Their mistakes, so we don't repeat them:**
  - Off-by-one band formulas (`TI − 24001`, `TI − 32334`, `TI − 800001`); at exactly
    TI = 24,000 neither band-1 condition fires.
  - Stale rates: NSSF 8,000/72,000 (2025, now 9,000/108,000), pension cap 20,000 (now 30,000),
    director rate 30%.
  - SHIF and Housing Levy charged on a partial base (no house or transport allowance, no
    overtime), and no SHIF KES 300 minimum.
  - PAYE can go negative.
  - The two spreadsheets contradict each other: the component sheet still gives 15% insurance
    relief on SHIF (pre-December 2024 law), and its GROSSIT only sums bands 1–3.
  - "Flexibility" means editing many formulas consistently by hand, which visibly drifted.

## 3. Kenyan rules used (and how sure we are)

**Confirmed**, from KRA's public notice on the Tax Laws (Amendment) Act 2024 (in force
27 December 2024, applying from December 2024 PAYE):
- Deductions before tax: SHIF, Housing Levy, post-retirement medical fund (≤ 15,000/month),
  mortgage interest (≤ 30,000/month, Fourth Schedule lender, own home), and registered
  pension/provident/individual retirement fund contributions (≤ 30,000/month).
- Housing relief and post-retirement medical fund relief ended.
- Not taxable: benefits totalling under 60,000 a year (5,000/month); the first 5,000/month of
  employer meals; gratuity paid into a registered scheme up to 360,000 per year of service.
- Statutory rates: NSSF Year 4 limits 9,000/108,000 at 6% + 6%; SHIF 2.75%, minimum 300;
  Housing Levy 1.5% + 1.5%; bands 24,000/32,333/500,000/800,000 at 10/25/30/32.5/35%;
  personal relief 2,400; NITA 50.

**Believed, not yet re-confirmed:**
- Insurance relief 15%, max 5,000/month (Income Tax Act s.31).
- Fringe benefit tax on below-market staff loans: paid by the employer at 30% of the interest
  the employee didn't pay, using KRA's quarterly prescribed rate.
- One-third rule: deductions may not exceed two-thirds of wages (Employment Act s.19(3)).
- Companies Act 2015 requires members' approval for loans to directors of public companies.

**Unconfirmed (these are settings, not code):**
- Secondary employment rate (35% used).
- Disability exemption amount: the Persons with Disabilities Act 2025 leaves it to the
  exemption granted (150,000 used as the default).
- Per diem limit (10,000/day reported; not modelled).

## 3b. Checked against KRA's own documents (2026-10-22)

Sources: KRA "PAYE as you earn" guide (2025/26 edition,
`kra.go.ke/images/publications/PAYE-AS-YOU-EARN-PAYE_4-01-2025.pdf`) and KRA's P9A template
(`P9-FORM-Template-2025.pdf`, created 25 Feb 2025).

- **Confirmed by KRA**: bands 24,000 / 8,333 / 467,667 / 300,000 at 10/25/30/32.5/35%; personal
  relief 2,400 a month (resident individuals); insurance relief 15% of premiums, max 5,000 a
  month (life, health or education, self/spouse/child); deductions for AHL, SHIF, PRMF (15,000),
  mortgage interest (30,000) and pension (30,000) from December 2024; meals tax-free up to
  5,000 a month (only the excess is taxable); PAYE and the return due by the 9th.
- **Fixed in the engine as a result**:
  - Pension allowed = lowest of **30% of basic salary** (P9A E1), actual contributions (E2)
    and 30,000 (E3). Was 30,000 only. New rates field `pension_limit_percent_of_basic`.
  - Non-cash benefits taxable only when they **exceed** 5,000 a month (was "at or above").
- **Recorded, not modelled yet**:
  - Night-out (per diem) allowance not chargeable up to **2,000 a day** per the KRA guide.
  - Employer pension contributions above 30,000 a month are a taxable benefit to the employee.
  - Personal relief is for resident individuals only (a non-resident category would get none).
  - Mileage above AA Kenya rates and club fees (where the employer deducted them) are taxable.
  - Penalties, useful for the reminders feature: late filing = higher of 25% of tax or 10,000;
    late payment = 5% plus 1% a month interest.
- **P9A layout (2025)**: A Basic, B Benefits non-cash, C Value of quarters, D Total gross,
  E1/E2/E3 retirement (30% of A / actual / 30,000), F AHL, G SHIF, H PRMF, I Owner-occupied
  interest, J Total deductions (lower of E + F + G + H + I), K Chargeable pay (D - J), L Tax
  charged, M Personal relief, N Insurance relief, O PAYE (L - M - N). The statutory-exports
  branch should match this exactly.
- **Independent check**: public calculators give KES 100,000 gross -> taxable 89,750 -> tax
  21,708 -> PAYE 19,308, identical to the engine.

## 3c. Filing formats (checked 2026-10-22)

**KRA simplified PAYE return** (mandatory from the July 2025 return; KRA "Step by step guide
for the simplified PAYE return", `kra.go.ke/images/publications/STEP-BY-STEP-GUIDE-FOR-THE-SIMPLIFIED-PAYE-RETURN.pdf`).
The Excel template and sample CSVs are on kra.go.ke/publications (JavaScript-listed, not
fetchable by script) or in the employer's iTax profile; **exact column order still needed**.
- Sheet A basic info: PIN, original/amended, head office/branch, period, whether there are
  gratuity / fringe benefit / lump sum / arrears.
- Sheet B employees: PIN, name, resident status, PWD; **Total Cash Pay** (basic, house,
  transport, leave pay, overtime, director's fee, lump sum, other allowances collapsed into one);
  car benefit; meals (excess over 5,000 taxed); non-cash benefits (all taxed above 5,000);
  housing value; other benefits; SHIF; NSSF + other pension (allowed up to 30,000); PRMF (15,000);
  mortgage interest (30,000); AHL; personal relief (2,400, **resident primary employees only**);
  insurance relief (**premiums deducted through payroll only**, 15%, max 5,000); self-assessed PAYE.
  The template computes PAYE itself and flags differences from the self-assessed figure.
- Sheet C lump sums: lump sum (3 contract categories), gratuity/severance (into a pension
  scheme, or paid to the employee and spread back up to 5 years), arrears (by year and month).
- Sheet D fringe benefit tax: PIN, loan account, loan amount, outstanding, loan rate, market rate.
- Sheet E tax due: totals; PAYE of employees without a PIN; AHL total (employee + employer);
  NITA = 50 x employees (primary, secondary, resident and non-resident).
- PAYE paid under Income Tax; AHL and NITA as "agency revenue"; paybill 222222, PRN as account.
- **Engine change made from this**: insurance relief only from payroll Insurance Premium
  deductions; the Employee "premiums paid directly" field was removed.

**NSSF** (official, `selfservice.nssf.or.ke`, "Payroll Template-Rel03.02.18.xlsx"): one sheet,
header row only then data: PAYROLL NUMBER, SURNAME, OTHER NAMES, ID NO, KRA PIN, NSSF NO,
GROSS PAY, VOLUNTARY. NSSF computes the contributions. Rules: no formulas, no special
characters, nothing after the last record, columns A-F as text, no blank or hidden rows, NSSF
number 9-10 characters with leading zeros / trailing X. (The older "Return Specifications"
PDF with contribution-type rows is the 2014 SF24 format.)

**SHA (SHIF)**: template only inside the employer portal (login). Third-party sources mention
SHA number, ID/identity type, gross salary, contribution. **Needed from the user.**

## 5. Casual and weekly-paid workers (2026-10-22)

**Decision (user, 2026-10-22):** monthly pay first. Casual workers paid monthly on a daily
rate are supported from v16.6.0 (`{ABBR} Kenya Casual Wages`: daily rate x Days Worked).
Weekly/daily pay is parked; Kenya Payroll structures must be Monthly (engine and structure
validation refuse anything else). Weekly pay is a niche in the SME market: concentrated in
construction, some farm and factory casual work, often paid outside any payroll system.

**What KRA says (checked):**
- *Monthly aggregation.* KRA Employer's Guide to PAYE (2017 revised edition, Part VI §4,
  "Irregularly paid employees"): "if employees are paid weekly, fortnightly or at any other
  interval, the entry of pay on the Tax Deduction Card for the month will be the total of
  payments in the month ... Tax due on the whole of the monthly pay will be deducted from the
  last payment." So weekly pay is NOT taxed on bands scaled to a week; the month's total is
  taxed on the monthly bands. The handoff's "scale bands per period" idea is wrong.
- *Who is "casual" for PAYE.* Same guide, Part I: "casual employment" = an engagement with one
  employer for less than one month, paid by reference to the engagement period or shorter
  intervals; its earnings are outside PAYE. "Regular part-time employees and regular casual
  employment where the employees are employed casually but regularly are not considered to be
  casual employees." Most SME "casuals" are regular, so PAYE applies in full.
- *Housing Levy.* Applies to casuals, including weekly wages (KRA, reported March 2024).
- *SHIF.* 2.75%, minimum KES 300 **per month** (SHI (General) Regulations 2024, per law-firm
  summaries; the Regulations' text not read directly).
- *NSSF.* Casuals are covered; limits are monthly (LEL 9,000 / UEL 108,000 from Feb 2026).

**Design for weekly pay, when needed (not built):** month-to-date. For each slip, add up this
slip and the employee's earlier submitted Kenya slips in the same calendar month, run the
existing monthly `compute()` on the total, and write each statutory amount as the month's
figure minus what earlier slips already deducted. Monthly staff are unchanged (no earlier
slips); the SHIF minimum, relief, NSSF limits and NITA apply once a month; a second slip in
the same month (e.g. a bonus run) is also taxed right, which it isn't today. Open choices:
group by period end date (recommended) or posting date; block a later slip while an earlier
one in the same month is still draft (recommended).

**For the tax adviser:** is PAYE deducted on genuine one-off casuals (engaged under a month)
today, given the 2017 guide's exclusion? Kenya Payroll deducts everything (the safer error).
